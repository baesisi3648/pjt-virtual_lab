[Critique Rubric — NGT Food Safety Assessment Document Review]

This rubric is used by the Scientific Critic agent to score specialist contributions. Each axis is scored on a 1–5 scale.

1. Scientific Evidence — 1–5
   [1] No scientific support; only ungrounded generalities.
   [2] Indirect references for some claims, but interpretive logic is weak.
   [3] Major claims are contextualised scientifically, and the necessity of the assessment emerges naturally from the argument.
   [4] Meets [3] + balances conceptual explanation with illustrative analytical approaches, achieving interpretive depth.
   [5] Meets [4] + considers alternative interpretations and competing perspectives, achieving logical completeness.

2. Universality — 1–5
   [1] Applicable only to a specific product/crop.
   [2] Applicable to some categories but skewed toward a particular trait.
   [3] Provides a conceptual frame applicable across NGT food categories.
   [4] Meets [3] + offers differentiated conceptual perspectives for SDN-1/SDN-2/SDN-3.
   [5] Meets [4] + presents a generalisable framework with the flexibility to extend to new NGT modalities and adjacent contexts.

3. Proportionality — 1–5
   [1] Data requirements are either excessively stringent or insufficient for the identified hazards; reads like a detailed regulatory protocol or omits assessments without justification.
   [2] Some sections are over-prescriptive, burdened with unnecessary technical detail, or omit an assessment without a stated scientific rationale.
   [3] Proposes a reasonable, conceptually framed approach proportionate to the scale and nature of the modification.
   [4] Meets [3] + justifies the proportionality principle interpretively, with scientific rationale for any simplification.
   [5] Meets [4] + considers consistency with international trends and discusses the balance between innovation and safety in interpretive terms.

4. Readability — 1–5
   [1] Many unexplained acronyms or graduate-level terms; numerical or protocol detail is excessive.
   [2] Some terms lack explanation; jargon is used without simplification.
   [3] All acronyms are expanded with gloss; difficult terms carry parenthetical explanations; numbers are absent.
   [4] Meets [3] + plain undergraduate-level phrasing allowing a non-specialist to follow the logic; no awkward "Why is X needed." transitions.
   [5] Meets [4] + understandable without prior background; necessity and context are fully woven into the prose.

[Deductions]
- Unexplained acronyms or jargon → deduct on Axis 4.
- Graduate-level vocabulary (phenome, haplotype, etc.) without gloss → deduct on Axis 4.
- Inclusion of numbers, thresholds, QC metrics, or protocols → deduct on Axes 3 and 4.
- Independent rhetorical-question transitions ("Why is X needed.") → deduct on Axis 4.
- Mere repetition of declarative sentences ("must be considered") without substantive content → deduct on Axis 1.

[Approval Threshold]
A score of 3 or higher on every axis is required for APPROVE. Any axis at 2 or lower triggers REVISE, with a concrete improvement direction articulated for that axis.

[Revision Templates]
- "Add the expansion and a one-line gloss to acronym X."
- "Replace term X with a plain-language equivalent or follow it with a parenthetical gloss."
- "Remove numerical specifics and re-express the point conceptually."
- "Eliminate the rhetorical-question transition and weave the necessity into the surrounding argument."
