[Academic Tone & Style Guide]

1. Terminology Calibration
   - Prefer plain, undergraduate-level phrasing over graduate-level jargon.
   - When technical terms are unavoidable, follow them with a short parenthetical explanation in everyday language.
     e.g. "haplotype (a set of variants inherited together on a single chromosome)"
     e.g. "epitope (the specific region of a protein recognised by an antibody)"
   - On first use, every acronym must be expanded with a brief gloss.
     e.g. "TTC (Threshold of Toxicological Concern), a conceptual threshold below which a substance is generally considered to pose negligible health concern."
   - Avoid the following terms unless accompanied by an accessible explanation: phenome, haplotype, process signature, molecular footprint, off-target profiling.

2. Exclude Numerical Specifics
   - Do not include numerical thresholds, ranges, ratios, detection limits, or accuracy figures.
   - Do not include experimental conditions, instrument settings, or step-by-step protocols.
   - You may name techniques as illustrative examples, but do not give numerical parameters.

3. Natural Logical Flow
   - The need for, and context of, each assessment should emerge naturally from the prose.
   - The reader should understand "why this matters" by following the argument, not by being told it as a meta-statement.
   - Use scholarly hedging where appropriate: "may be interpreted as…", "can be considered in the context of…".

4. Forbidden Constructions
   - Normative phrasing: "must", "required", "threshold", "acceptance criterion".
   - Sentence structures that read like a regulatory directive.
   - ★ Independent rhetorical questions used as section transitions, e.g. "Why is X needed.", "Why is this classification necessary."
     → Convey necessity through the flow of the argument itself, never through a meta-question.
   - Exception: the prohibition on normative phrasing does not apply to wording that flags uncertainty, requests additional data, or defers a decision (e.g. "Additional data required", "Decision deferred pending further data").
